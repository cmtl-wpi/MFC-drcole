Physics / film-model critique of the bouncing design doc. Most-important first. I verified the dimensional and numeric claims by hand where noted; I did not need the MFC source for a physics review.

---

**P1 — The injected-force closure (flat-film Stefan–Reynolds) is structurally incompatible with the physics the doc itself sets as validation targets.**
Section 3.4 injects `p_film(r) = 3μ_g V_n (a²−r²)/h³`: a single scalar `h`, pressure *maximum at the center* (`r=0`), monotonically decreasing to zero at the rim. But the load-bearing physics the doc lists (Section 1.3, Rung 4/5) is the *dimpled* film: film thicker at center, `h_min` at the **rim**, pressure **peaking at the rim**, negative gauge pressure near the rim during bounce-off (Fig 15b). Stefan–Reynolds flat-film cannot produce a dimple, a rim minimum, a rim pressure peak, or a nonmonotonic `h_min` — it produces the qualitative opposite. Rung 4 and Rung 5 will therefore fail by construction with this closure. Worse, it is a *different* lubrication theory from the spatially-resolved Reynolds PDE in Section 3.3, so the force model and the thickness model are two mutually inconsistent lubrication descriptions.
**Fix:** use ONE consistent Reynolds description. Solve the film pressure from the *same* Reynolds equation used to evolve `h` (invert `∇·[(ρh³/12μ)∇_t p] = ∂(ρh)/∂t + Q·∇_t(ρh)` for `p` on the band, as Musehane actually does), and inject *that* dimpled pressure field. Demote the analytic Stefan–Reynolds formula to a flat-plate sanity check only. Without this you get neither the dimple diagnostics nor the merge mechanism (see P4).

**P2 — The doc misidentifies the restoring force. The lubrication force is dissipative and vanishes exactly when the bounce turns over; the actual spring is capillary drop deformation, which is resolved.**
`p_film ∝ V_n`. At maximum compression `V_n = 0`, so `p_film = 0` — the film contributes *zero* force at the turning point. The rebound is initiated entirely by the resolved surface-tension energy stored in the deformed drop (deformation scale ~`D`, well resolved at `D/200`). During separation `V_n < 0` and `p_film < 0`: the film *sucks the drops back together* (this is physically the Fig-15b negative rim pressure), i.e. the lubrication force *opposes* rebound. So framing the injected force as "the rebound force" that MFC must add "to supply the repulsion" (Sections 3.2, 4.1b, R1) is conceptually wrong. The film's real jobs are (a) transmit load so the drops decelerate and deform correctly, and (b) gate merging via `h(t)` vs `h_crit`. The good news: because the capillary spring is resolved, bouncing *can* emerge — but for the right reason, not the one stated.
**Fix:** rewrite Section 3.2/R1 around "the injected force supplies the missing sub-grid *deceleration/load-transmission* and prevents premature numerical merge," not "rebound." Change the R1 decision-gate metric from "is `τ_b` under-predicted / is rebound adequate" to "do the drops merge prematurely / is the deceleration and `h(t)` correct." The rebound test is a *capillary* test.

**P3 — It is never specified which pressure gradient drives the drainage, and the two candidates give completely different `h(t)`.**
Section 3.3 drains `h` via `∇_t p`; R6b says feed it the *resolved* pressure minus the σ-jump. But the resolved `D/200` pressure field is exactly the one missing the sub-grid film pressure — the whole premise of Section 3.2. So draining `h` with the resolved `∇p` uses a pressure that lacks the lubrication pressure that physically drives the drainage. The alternative — drain with the injected `p_film` — forces 3.3 and 3.4 to be the same field (which they currently are not, per P1). As written the drainage driver is underdetermined.
**Fix:** the self-consistent choice is P1's coupled solve: the same Reynolds pressure both drains `h` and is injected as the force. Then the drainage driver, the injected force, and the diagnostic pressure are one object.

**P4 — The nonmonotonic bounce→merge (region II→III, `We≈10.3`) does not follow from `t_drain ∝ a⁴`; that scaling predicts *monotonic* bounce at high We.**
Section 3.5 claims merge→bounce→merge "emerges from `t_drain ∝ a⁴`." The first transition (merge→bounce) is fine: higher We → larger lamella `a` → longer drainage → bounce. But if `a` keeps growing with We and `t_drain ∝ a⁴`, drainage time keeps increasing, which predicts drops stay bounced at high We — the opposite of the II→III re-merge. The real II→III mechanism is that increased inertia drives the **rim** `h_min` below `h_crit` (the rim thins faster than the wider lamella can compensate) *while still pressed*. That is a competition of `h_min(t)` against `h_crit`, driven by rim thinning — precisely the dimple structure the flat-film closure cannot represent (P1). So S2, the doc's own "load-bearing physics," cannot be reproduced with the stated model.
**Fix:** the criterion must be `min_rim h(t) < h_crit` on the *dimpled* field from P1's coupled Reynolds solve, tracking the rim minimum, not a single flat `h`. State the II→III mechanism explicitly as rim-thinning, and confirm the closure can produce a rim minimum before claiming S2.

**P5 — Magnitude and stiffness: the closure over-predicts film pressure by orders of magnitude unless `V_n` is the tiny instantaneous value, and the `1/h³` explicit source is stiff.**
Plugging nominal numbers into `p_film = 3μ_g V_n a²/h³` at the dimple (`μ_g=1.8e-5`, `V_n=0.5`, `a≈90µm`, `h=0.3µm`) gives ~**8 MPa** — four orders above the physical film pressure, which the doc's own Fig-16 target says is `~ρU_r² ≈ O(10²–10³ Pa)`. The resolution is that by the time `h~0.3µm` the drop has already decelerated, so the *instantaneous resolved* `V_n ≪ U/2`; the pressure is inertially capped at `~ρU²` by drop deformation, not by viscous drainage. Note the mismatch of scalings: the injected closure scales *viscously* (`μ_g/h³`), the validation target (Fig 16 collapse) scales *inertially* (`ρU_r²`). If you inject the viscous formula with anything but the correct tiny instantaneous `V_n`, you both violate the Fig-16 collapse and dump ~MPa into a cell in one 9e-10 s acoustic step → instability. `p_film ∝ 1/h³` spanning two decades of `h` (1.5µm→30nm) is a genuinely stiff explicit source the doc never addresses.
**Fixes:** (a) use the resolved instantaneous `V_n` with the two-way feedback (force decelerates drop → `V_n→0` → `p→0`) so it self-limits; (b) add an explicit magnitude cross-check that peak injected `p` collapses on `ρU_r²` (this IS Rung 5 — make it a stability guard, not just a validation output); (c) cap/limit or sub-cycle the source, or treat it semi-implicitly, or you will get acoustic blow-ups as `h→h_crit`.

**P6 — Sub-grid viscous dissipation is misrepresented; the bounce may be too elastic and energy-inconsistent.**
Injecting `p_film·n̂` on momentum plus `u·f_film` on energy treats the film force as if it does reversible work on the *resolved* cell. But the physical lubrication resistance *dissipates* energy inside the sub-grid film (that dissipation is why bouncing is lossy and `τ_b/τ_o < 1`). Simply adding `u·f_film` to the resolved total-energy RHS does not put that dissipation anywhere physical — at best it recirculates mechanical work between resolved liquid and gas, at worst it makes the bounce artificially elastic (`τ_b` too long, restitution too high). The thermodynamic partition (mechanical work on the drop vs. viscous heating of the unresolved film gas) is unspecified.
**Fix:** specify that the sub-grid lubrication dissipation `~ M·∇_t p` (Poiseuille dissipation) is deposited as internal energy in the band gas cells, distinct from the mechanical work term. Validate against `τ_b/τ_o = 0.29 We^{-0.35}+0.88` (Eq 13) — restitution is the direct test that dissipation is right.

**P7 — Rarefaction `f(Kn)=1+6Kn` is used well outside its validity and it directly moves the merge thresholds, so it is not a harmless placeholder.**
First-order slip is valid `Kn ≲ 0.1`. At the dimple `Kn≈0.3` (marginal, `f≈2.8`) and at rupture `Kn≈3.3` (`f≈20`, meaningless — that's transition/free-molecular). Because `f(Kn)` rescales `μ_g` in *both* the force and the drainage flux, it directly sets how fast `h` reaches `h_crit`, i.e. it directly sets the We≈1.88/10.3 transitions. So "placeholder flagged for calibration" understates it: S2 outcomes are a strong function of this unknown function.
**Fix:** get the actual `Q(Kn)`/`f(Kn)` from Zhang & Law or the 2025 rarefied dual-VOF papers before running S2, and report S2 sensitivity to `f(Kn)`. Also: `λ≈100nm` for N₂ at 1 atm is high (STP value ~66nm); using 100nm inflates `Kn` by ~1.5× — pin this down since it feeds the threshold.

**P8 — The Qian & Law `We≈1.88 / 10.3` transition targets are pressure- and system-specific; using them may be validating against the wrong numbers.**
Qian & Law's central result is that bouncing appears/disappears with *gas pressure* — the region-II window is a strong function of ambient pressure and gas. The 1.88/10.3 values come from a specific condition. The doc mixes them with Chen & Yang's tetradecane/N₂-1atm system without confirming they are the same system/pressure. If not, S2's pass/fail is anchored to the wrong thresholds.
**Fix:** take the S2 transition We from Chen & Yang's own regime map (or Qian & Law at the *matching* gas/pressure), and cite that condition explicitly.

**P9 — Sign convention of the injected force is unstated and easy to get backwards.**
`f_film = p_film n̂` is repulsive only if `n̂` is the liquid-inward normal (`∝ +∇α`), so `p>0` pushes the interface into the drop (away from the film). With the gas-film-outward normal it would be attractive and collapse the drops. Mason's `∇F` is liquid-inward; state this explicitly for each marker, since the two markers have opposite normals across the gap.
**Fix:** define `n̂_θ = ∇α_θ/|∇α_θ|` (into liquid θ) and verify the two injected forces are anti-parallel (drops pushed apart) in a unit test before Phase 2.

**What checks out (so you don't churn on it):**
- The `ρh` Reynolds equation (3.3) is dimensionally consistent (I verified: all terms `kg m⁻² s⁻¹`), and the compressible-conservative form is the right choice for a gas film (though real film `ΔP~10²Pa` makes gas compressibility negligible — it's harmless over-engineering).
- The velocity relabeling and `τ_b` self-consistency hold: `U_r≈0.99 m/s` reproduces `We=8.6`; Eq 10 and Eq 11 both give `τ_b≈745µs`, `τ_c≈309µs`, `τ_o≈713µs` — mutually consistent (I recomputed). Minor: `We=8.6` back-solves to `U=0.99` but `Re=105.9` back-solves to `U=0.97`; a ~2% internal inconsistency, within property uncertainty, but don't advertise it as exact.
- Omitting vdW from the drainage driver and using `h_crit` as a rupture surrogate is a standard, defensible modeling choice (Mason, Kwakkel) — keep the R3 caveat that the *merge* outcome is sensitive to `h_crit` below ~50nm.
- The `t_drain ∝ a⁴` / `F ∝ 1/h³` scalings and the `V_drain=V_n` self-consistency of the Stefan–Reynolds pair are algebraically correct.
- The feasibility/step-count argument (Section 2) is sound: I confirmed `dt≈9.2ps` at 15nm → `~1.6e8` steps, `~1.6e6` at `D/200`.

**One opportunity:** for head-on S1/S2 you can impose a reflective symmetry plane at the collision midplane. That plane physically holds the two drops apart and enforces `V_n` symmetry for free, halving cost and side-stepping the Phase-1 "does single-color surface tension hold the markers apart" risk (R9) entirely for the head-on rungs. Reserve the two-marker anti-merge machinery for S3, where you genuinely have no symmetry plane. This also isolates whether your bounce comes from real physics vs. the marker trick.

**Bottom line:** bouncing *can* emerge from this architecture, but for a reason the doc under-credits (resolved capillary spring) and despite a force closure (flat-film Stefan–Reynolds) that is inconsistent with the drainage model, the dimple/rim diagnostics, the `ρU²` pressure collapse, and the high-We re-merge mechanism. Collapse 3.3 and 3.4 into a single coupled Reynolds solve for a dimpled pressure, drive drainage and the injected force from that one field, track the rim `h_min`, use the instantaneous resolved `V_n` with a stiffness limiter, and account for sub-grid dissipation. Those five changes are what make S2, Rung 4, and Rung 5 physically reachable.
